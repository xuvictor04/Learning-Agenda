# Mind

## Neuroscience
- **Survey:** *The Brain: The Story of You* — David Eagleman.
- **Canon:** *The Man Who Mistook His Wife for a Hat* — Oliver Sacks (the
  mind revealed through its breakdowns); *Principles of Neural Science* —
  Kandel et al. as the field's reference bible when you go deep.
- **Course:** MIT OCW 9.13 *The Human Brain* (Nancy Kanwisher), free and
  superb.

## Psychology (cognitive, social, developmental, clinical)
- **Survey:** *Thinking, Fast and Slow* — Daniel Kahneman (cognition and
  judgment) and *Psychology* — Peter Gray as a real textbook survey.
- **Canon:** William James, *The Principles of Psychology* (1890 — still
  astonishing); *Man's Search for Meaning* — Viktor Frankl for the clinical/
  existential side.
- **Course:** Yale's *Introduction to Psychology* — Paul Bloom (free on
  Coursera / Open Yale PSYC 110), one of the best intro courses anywhere.

## Linguistics
- **Survey:** *The Language Instinct* — Steven Pinker.
- **Canon:** Ferdinand de Saussure, *Course in General Linguistics* (founding
  text of modern linguistics); Chomsky's *Syntactic Structures* for the
  generative turn.
- **Course:** MIT OCW 24.900 *Introduction to Linguistics*.

## Artificial intelligence (as a field of study)
- **Survey:** *Artificial Intelligence: A Guide for Thinking Humans* —
  Melanie Mitchell (what AI is, honestly, without hype).
- **Canon:** Turing, *Computing Machinery and Intelligence* (1950 — the
  imitation game paper); *Artificial Intelligence: A Modern Approach* —
  Russell & Norvig as the field's standard reference.
- **Course:** Andrew Ng's *Machine Learning Specialization* (Coursera) for
  the modern practice; fast.ai's *Practical Deep Learning* (free) if you
  want to build. For the concepts alone, 3Blue1Brown's neural-network series.
