# Human & Social

## World history (deep-time to modern)
- **Survey:** *Sapiens* — Yuval Noah Harari (one arc from foragers to now),
  balanced by *An Illustrated History of the World* or the *Very Short
  Introduction* histories for facts to hang on the story.
- **Canon:** *The Histories* — Herodotus (history's founding book); for the
  modern era, Eric Hobsbawm's *Age of…* quartet.
- **Course:** *Big History Project* (free) for the deep-time frame; Open Yale
  has strong period courses (e.g. HIST 210 *The Early Middle Ages*).

## Anthropology & archaeology
- **Survey:** *The Dawn of Everything* — Graeber & Wengrow (a bracing rethink
  of human social history).
- **Canon:** *Tristes Tropiques* — Claude Lévi-Strauss; *Coming of Age in
  Samoa* — Margaret Mead (read with its later critiques).
- **Course:** MIT OCW 21A anthropology offerings; Stanford's *Human
  Behavioral Biology* (Sapolsky) overlaps richly.

## Sociology
- **Survey:** *The Sociological Imagination* — C. Wright Mills (short, and the
  discipline's mission statement).
- **Canon:** Durkheim, *Suicide*; Weber, *The Protestant Ethic and the Spirit
  of Capitalism*; Du Bois, *The Souls of Black Folk*.
- **Course:** any open *Introduction to Sociology*; the OpenStax textbook is
  free and solid.

## Political science
- **Survey:** *Politics: A Very Short Introduction* — Kenneth Minogue.
- **Canon:** Machiavelli, *The Prince*; Hobbes, *Leviathan* (intro + Part I);
  *The Federalist Papers* (a selection).
- **Course:** Harvard's *Justice* — Michael Sandel (free; more political
  philosophy) paired with a comparative-government intro.

## Law & legal systems
- **Survey:** *Law 101* — Jay Feinman.
- **Canon:** H.L.A. Hart, *The Concept of Law*; for common-law reasoning,
  *A Concise Restatement* is dry — prefer the case method via *Getting to
  Maybe* or reading a few landmark opinions.
- **Course:** Yale's *The Constitution* offerings; the *Introduction to
  American Law* (Penn, Coursera, free).

## Economics
- **Survey:** *The Economics Book* (DK) for the map; then *Naked Economics* —
  Charles Wheelan.
- **Canon:** Adam Smith, *The Wealth of Nations* (Book I); Keynes, *The
  General Theory* (hard — read *about* it via Heilbroner first).
- **Course:** **CORE Econ**, *The Economy* — free, modern, exceptional
  (core-econ.org); or MIT OCW 14.01 *Principles of Microeconomics*.
- **Reference:** *The Worldly Philosophers* — Robert Heilbroner (the great
  economists as a story; read this first, honestly).

## Finance & markets
- **Survey:** *The Ascent of Money* — Niall Ferguson.
- **Canon:** *A Random Walk Down Wall Street* — Burton Malkiel; *The
  Intelligent Investor* — Benjamin Graham.
- **Course:** Yale's *Financial Markets* — Robert Shiller (free on Coursera /
  Open Yale ECON 252), a Nobel laureate teaching the whole system.

## Geography & geopolitics
- **Survey:** *Prisoners of Geography* — Tim Marshall.
- **Canon:** Halford Mackinder, *The Geographical Pivot of History* (1904
  essay); *Guns, Germs, and Steel* — Jared Diamond (read with its critiques).
- **Course:** open *Human Geography* intros; National Geographic and the
  *Very Short Introductions* fill gaps well.

## Education (how humans learn / how systems teach)
- **Survey:** *Make It Stick* — Brown, Roediger & McDaniel (the science of
  learning — directly useful to this whole agenda).
- **Canon:** *Pedagogy of the Oppressed* — Paulo Freire; Dewey, *Democracy
  and Education*.
- **Course:** *Learning How to Learn* — Barbara Oakley (Coursera, free; the
  most-enrolled course in the world for good reason).

## Media & communication
- **Survey:** *Understanding Media* — Marshall McLuhan (the medium is the
  message), or *Amusing Ourselves to Death* — Neil Postman for accessibility.
- **Canon:** Walter Lippmann, *Public Opinion* (1922); *Manufacturing
  Consent* — Herman & Chomsky.
- **Course:** open media-studies intros; pair with Postman above.
