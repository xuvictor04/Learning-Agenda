# Physical

## Physics
- **Survey:** *Seven Brief Lessons on Physics* — Carlo Rovelli (an evening's
  read, a map of the territory); then *The Theoretical Minimum* — Leonard
  Susskind & George Hrabovsky, when you want the math to be real.
- **Canon:** *The Feynman Lectures on Physics* — free in full at
  feynmanlectures.caltech.edu. Read Volume I slowly over a year; it's the
  canon *and* the survey for anyone with calculus.
- **Course:** MIT OCW 8.01/8.02 (Walter Lewin's classic lectures survive on
  YouTube); Susskind's *Theoretical Minimum* Stanford lecture series (free)
  goes all the way to general relativity for committed amateurs.

## Cosmology & astronomy
- **Survey:** *Cosmos* — Carl Sagan (book and/or series); still the best
  single invitation to the universe.
- **Canon:** *A Brief History of Time* — Stephen Hawking.
- **Course:** Open Yale ASTR 160, *Frontiers and Controversies in
  Astrophysics* (Charles Bailyn) — exoplanets, black holes, dark energy.

## Chemistry
- **Survey:** *The Disappearing Spoon* — Sam Kean. The periodic table as
  biography.
- **Canon:** *The Periodic Table* — Primo Levi (chemistry as literature; one
  of the great books of the century) and Linus Pauling's *General Chemistry*
  (Dover) for the science in a master's voice.
- **Course:** MIT OCW 5.111 *Principles of Chemical Science*.

## Earth science & climate
- **Survey:** *Origins: How Earth's History Shaped Human History* — Lewis
  Dartnell. Plate tectonics to politics in one arc.
- **Canon:** the IPCC Assessment Report *Summary for Policymakers* (latest
  cycle; free) — the reference document of the climate era. Historical
  canon: John McPhee's *Annals of the Former World* (Pulitzer; geology as
  epic).
- **Course:** *Global Warming I: The Science and Modeling of Climate Change*
  — David Archer, University of Chicago (free on Coursera), with his short
  book *Global Warming: Understanding the Forecast*.

## Materials science
- **Survey:** *Stuff Matters* — Mark Miodownik. Ten materials, brilliantly told.
- **Canon:** *The New Science of Strong Materials* — J.E. Gordon (the classic
  that created the popular genre).
- **Course:** MIT OCW 3.091 *Introduction to Solid-State Chemistry* (Donald
  Sadoway's lectures).
