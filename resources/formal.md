# Formal

## Mathematics (as a domain)
- **Survey:** *Mathematics: Its Content, Methods and Meaning* — Aleksandrov,
  Kolmogorov & Lavrent'ev. The great panorama of the subject, written by
  masters; Dover keeps it in print cheap.
- **Canon:** Euclid, *Elements* (Book I at minimum) — the founding document of
  proof itself. Pair with *Journey Through Genius* (William Dunham) to tour
  the other landmark theorems with context.
- **Course:** 3Blue1Brown's *Essence of Linear Algebra* and *Essence of
  Calculus* video series for intuition; MIT OCW 18.01/18.06 (Gilbert
  Strang's linear algebra lectures are legendary) when you want the real thing.
- **Reference:** *The Princeton Companion to Mathematics* (ed. Timothy
  Gowers) — the whole field in one (large) volume.

## Logic & foundations
- **Survey:** *Logic: A Very Short Introduction* — Graham Priest.
- **Canon:** *Gödel's Proof* — Nagel & Newman (the incompleteness theorems,
  honestly explained in 100 pages). Then *Gödel, Escher, Bach* (Hofstadter)
  if the subject bites you.
- **Course:** *Introduction to Logic* — Stanford's open course (Genesereth),
  on Coursera; or Peter Smith's free *An Introduction to Formal Logic* +
  his *Teach Yourself Logic* study guide (free PDF) for the road onward.

## Statistics & probability
- **Survey:** *The Art of Statistics* — David Spiegelhalter. Statistical
  thinking with real cases, almost no formulas wasted.
- **Canon/Text:** *Introduction to Probability* — Blitzstein & Hwang (free
  online), the book behind Harvard's Stat 110.
- **Course:** Harvard **Stat 110** (Joe Blitzstein) — all lectures free on
  YouTube/edX. For the Bayesian road later: *Statistical Rethinking*
  (McElreath), whose lectures are also free.

## Theoretical computer science & information theory
- **Survey:** *Code: The Hidden Language of Computer Hardware and Software* —
  Charles Petzold. From flashlight signals to a working computer; the best
  on-ramp to what computation *is*.
- **Canon:** Claude Shannon, *A Mathematical Theory of Communication* (1948
  paper — readable with Stat 110 under your belt); Alan Turing, *On
  Computable Numbers* (1936) with a guide such as *The Annotated Turing*
  (Petzold).
- **Course/Text:** *Introduction to the Theory of Computation* — Michael
  Sipser (the standard text, beautifully written); MIT OCW 18.404 is
  Sipser teaching his own book.
