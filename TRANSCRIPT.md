# Transcript

A degree-shaped progression built entirely from free university courses.
Eight terms, four per year, the way a real transcript runs — foundations
before methods, methods before depth.

**Everything here is free and open.** MIT OpenCourseWare, Open Yale Courses,
Harvard's public offerings, and a few standout lecture series that were
published for anyone.

**On course codes:** they drift, and departments renumber. Where I'm
confident of a code it's given; where I'm not, the course is named by title
and instructor, which is what you should search on anyway.

**Load:** three or four courses a term, ~10–14 hrs/week. A real full-time
load is five, but a real full-time student isn't also employed.

## Rules that make this a transcript rather than a playlist

1. **Problem sets are the course.** Lectures without them are television.
   MIT OCW ships the psets and solutions; do them before checking.
2. **One artifact per course** — a written summary, a solved problem set you'd
   show someone, or a built thing. No artifact, no credit.
3. **Prerequisites are real.** 18.06 after 18.01. 8.02 after 8.01. Ignore
   them and the course teaches nothing.
4. **Three or four at a time, always mixed.** Never four quantitative courses
   in one term.
5. **A dropped course gets logged, not hidden.** Dropping deliberately is a
   result.
