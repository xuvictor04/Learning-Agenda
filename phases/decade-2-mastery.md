# Decade 2 (Years 11–20): Mastery, the Second Spike, and Original Work

**Mission:** convert Decade 1's depth into mastery and original contribution;
bring the second spike to professional strength; become someone others learn
from. This is typically the decade of maximum career output — the agenda's job
is to keep you *learning* through it, because this is exactly when most people
stop.

## The central risk of Decade 2

Competence is comfortable. By now you're paid to do what you're already good
at, and every incentive says: repeat it. People who plateau in their 30s
usually didn't decide to stop learning — their calendar decided for them.
The countermeasure is structural, not motivational: keep a fixed weekly block
(≥5 hours) that is for learning at the edge of your ability, treat it like a
client meeting, and audit it in the annual review.

## Years 11–14: The π takes shape

**Themes**

- Push the second spike from foundations to working professional strength.
  The fastest route is the same as Decade 1's: fundamentals first, then real
  projects, ideally ones that already use your first spike (this is the π
  advantage — you can take on hybrid projects specialists in either field
  can't).
- In the first spike, move from *solving problems* to *choosing problems*:
  taste in what's worth working on is the master-level skill, and it's built
  by post-mortems, studying your field's history of bets, and proximity to
  people with proven taste.
- Start saying no. Mastery-level learning requires longer uninterrupted arcs;
  protect 1–2 deep commitments per year over 6 shallow ones.

**Milestones by year 14**

- [ ] Second spike at "trusted professional" level — you've shipped real work
      in it.
- [ ] First deliberately *hybrid* project completed at the intersection.
- [ ] Explicit personal answer to "what problems are worth my next five
      years?" — written down, revisited annually.

## Years 15–17: Original contribution

**Themes**

- Aim at work that wouldn't exist without you: research, a product, a body of
  writing, a practice or method, a company, a school of thought — whatever
  "original contribution" means in your fields. Originality is mostly
  earned recombination: the π-shape is your raw material.
- Learn the *meta-skills of scale*: leading teams, allocating capital or
  resources, judging people, negotiating, organizational design. Whatever
  your fields, impact at this stage flows through other people; these skills
  now have the highest marginal value and most people your age still lack
  them.
- Teach at a higher level: mentor several people seriously, speak, write for
  your field, maybe teach formally. Your explanations now carry experience
  that textbooks can't.

**Milestones by year 17**

- [ ] One piece of work you'd defend as an original contribution.
- [ ] Leading/mentoring others is a routine part of your week.
- [ ] You've taught your specialty's material at a serious level (course,
      book-in-progress, talk series, or long-form writing with an audience).

## Years 18–20: Consolidation and the long-game audit

**Themes**

- Consolidate: finish the long arcs, publish the accumulated knowledge,
  turn scattered expertise into legible artifacts (a book, a body of work, a
  named method, a team that runs without you).
- Run the **20-year audit** against `02-map.md`: Which bets paid off? What
  would you tell your year-1 self? What's the third act about? Decade 3
  planning starts here, and it may legitimately point somewhere new —
  reinvention chosen from strength, not boredom.
- Begin deliberately learning from people two levels down: fields move, and
  the young see the new thing first. Reverse-mentoring keeps your model of
  the field current when seniority starts insulating you from it.

**Milestones by end of the decade**

- [ ] A legible, durable body of work exists in at least one spike.
- [ ] 20-year audit written; Decade 3 direction drafted.
- [ ] Learning block still on the calendar, still defended, still used.

## Failure modes to watch for in Decade 2

- **The comfortable plateau** — repeating year 12 eight times and calling it
  eight years of experience.
- **Management amnesia** — moving into leadership and letting the craft
  atrophy completely. Keep ~20% hands-on practice in at least one spike;
  judgment decays without contact with the material.
- **Hoarding** — accumulating expertise without teaching or publishing it.
  Unshared mastery doesn't compound; it just retires with you.
- **Peak-earning-years capture** — every hour monetized, zero invested.
  The curiosity budget (see `02-map.md`) is the canary: when it hits zero,
  the agenda is dying.
