# Decade 3 (Years 21–30): Mastery and Original Work

**Mission:** bring the second spike to mastery, produce work that would not
exist without you, teach at a level textbooks can't reach, and build the first
real bridges between the fields the completed map put in reach.

The map has been covered since year 15 and the first spike has been at mastery
since late in Decade 2. What that combination buys is not authority — it is
material. Originality is mostly earned recombination, and by now you are one
of the few people carrying two fields of genuine depth *and* a walked map of
everything around them. This decade is where that stops being potential.

It is also, for most people, the decade of maximum professional output, which
is exactly when learning quietly stops. The countermeasure is structural, not
motivational: a fixed weekly block for work at the edge of your ability,
treated like a client meeting, audited at the annual review.

## Years 21–24: The second spike to mastery

**Themes**

- Push the second spike from professional strength to mastery: original work
  in it, held to the standard of the field's best rather than to your own
  first spike's standard for a newcomer. The route is the one that worked
  twice already — fundamentals, then real projects, then problems nobody
  handed you.
- Run the two spikes together deliberately. Hybrid projects that specialists
  in either field cannot take on are your comparative advantage, and they are
  the cheapest original work available to you.
- **Keep promoting, slowly.** Two or three more domains to working depth
  across this decade, one at a time, at reviews, under the caps in
  `02-map.md`. Kill criteria apply harder now: a promotion that hasn't
  produced real work in two years gets demoted at review rather than defended.
- Keep one hard, humbling practice alive in a spike — something with
  unfakeable feedback — so judgment stays calibrated and seniority doesn't
  curdle into pronouncement.

**Milestones by year 24**

- [ ] Second spike at mastery: work in it that the field's best will argue
      with rather than praise politely.
- [ ] Two or more hybrid projects completed at the intersection.
- [ ] One or two more domains promoted to working depth, and the tier table
      in `02-map.md` rewritten to say so.

## Years 25–27: Original contribution, and the first bridges

**Themes**

- Aim at work that wouldn't exist without you: research, a product, a body of
  writing, a practice or method, a company, a school of thought — whatever
  "original contribution" means in your fields.
- **Build the first bridges.** The cross-field questions you have been
  noticing since Decade 2 — the "someone should really connect these two
  worlds" projects — are now takeable. Name the two or three you are best
  positioned to ask and pick one. A completed ledger is what tells you which
  bridge has something on the far side of it.
- Learn the *meta-skills of scale*: leading teams, allocating capital or
  resources, judging people, negotiating, organizational design. Whatever
  your fields, impact at this stage flows through other people; these skills
  now have the highest marginal value and most people at your stage still
  lack them.

**Milestones by year 27**

- [ ] One piece of work you'd defend as an original contribution.
- [ ] One bridge project finished that neither spike alone could produce.
- [ ] Leading/mentoring others is a routine part of your week.

## Years 28–30: Teaching, the first re-foundation, and the audit

**Themes**

- **Teach at a higher level.** Mentor several people seriously, speak, write
  for your field, teach formally if you can. Your explanations now carry
  experience textbooks can't, and teaching is still the fastest way to find
  out what you only think you know.
- **Run the first systematic re-foundation pass.** The trunk domains were
  learned in years 1–10 and are now fifteen to thirty years old. Go row by row
  through `02-map.md` asking one question of each: *how far has this moved
  since I learned it?* Take the 5–8 with the largest answer and redo the
  literacy pass on current material — new survey, new canonical work, new
  artifact.
- The fields that move fastest are instrument-driven and computational:
  genetics, neuroscience, cosmology, materials, AI, climate, medicine —
  anywhere the data got orders of magnitude bigger or a new instrument opened
  a window shut when you studied. Textual domains drift slowly and can wait.
- **Be taught the new version by people who learned it after you did.** Normal
  and correct, not a concession. The generation that built a field's last
  fifteen years explains them better than anyone who watched from a distance,
  and your job in their classroom is to be a good student. Being publicly a
  beginner is also the best defense against intellectual rigidity, which is
  the real enemy from here on.
- Check `04-frontier.md` in the same sitting. Some of what looks like drift is
  a field that didn't exist when the map was written, and belongs in the
  frontier slot instead.

**Milestones by end of the decade**

- [ ] You've taught your specialty's material at a serious level (course,
      book-in-progress, talk series, or long-form writing with an audience).
- [ ] First re-foundation pass complete: 5–8 domains re-founded on current
      material, each with a new artifact.
- [ ] At least two of them learned from a teacher who entered the field after
      you did.
- [ ] Frontier slot funded and running, with something in it that has no
      ledger row.
- [ ] 7–10 domains at working depth in total, each with work you'd show a
      specialist.
- [ ] The year-30 review written, and Decade 4 drafted. Forty years left, two
      spikes at mastery, and a map that has now been walked once and repaired.

## Ongoing maintenance (all of Decade 3)

- **Habits, unchanged:** spaced repetition, weekly log, annual review. They
  are three decades old now; they cost almost nothing and are load-bearing.
  The SRS vault is carrying forty-four domains, which is precisely the job it
  was built for.
- **Cognitive health is curriculum:** exercise, sleep, social engagement, and
  sustained hard learning itself are the best-evidenced protectors of the
  instrument this whole agenda runs on. They stay on the schedule, and from
  Decade 4 they get scheduled explicitly.
- **The third spike is not this decade's business.** It belongs to Decade 5,
  chosen on pull alone. Noting candidates is free; funding one now costs the
  second spike its mastery.

## Failure modes to watch for in Decade 3

- **Eminence trap** — spending the decade giving opinions about work instead
  of doing any. The humbling-practice rule is the antidote.
- **Originality deferred** — waiting to feel ready. Nobody arrives at
  original work feeling qualified; you arrive at it by starting something
  nobody asked for and finishing it.
- **Hoarding** — accumulating expertise without teaching or publishing it.
  Unshared mastery doesn't compound; it retires with you.
- **Nostalgia curriculum** — teaching the field as it was in your prime. The
  re-foundation pass and reverse-mentoring keep the material current, which is
  why they're scheduled rather than intended.
- **Coasting disguised as balance** — legitimate slowing down chosen
  consciously is fine; the failure mode is drift that was never chosen.
  The annual review is where the difference gets written down.
