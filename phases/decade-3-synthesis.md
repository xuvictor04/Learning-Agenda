# Decade 3 (Years 21–30): Synthesis, Stewardship, Reinvention

**Mission:** do the work only someone with your accumulated spikes can do;
transfer what you know to people and institutions that outlast you; and keep
the learner alive — including, if chosen, by starting something genuinely new.

By now the agenda's early bets have matured: two-plus fields of real depth,
decades of artifacts, a network, and taste. The question of this phase is no
longer "how do I get good?" but "what is all this *for*?" The three tracks
below usually run in parallel, with the mix shifting over the decade.

**This is the hinge, not the end.** The 44-domain ledger completes near the
close of this decade — with forty years still to run. The three tracks
below are where the second half's work begins, and
`decade-4-integration.md` picks them up from here.

## Track A: Synthesis — the work of the third act

- Take on the problems that need your specific combination: the cross-field
  questions, the "someone should really connect these two worlds" projects
  you've been noticing since Decade 2. These are your comparative advantage
  and, for most people, the most satisfying work of their lives.
- Write the synthesis down at full scale — the book, the framework, the
  survey that maps a territory you're one of few to have walked end-to-end.
  Decade 3 is when field-defining syntheses tend to get written, precisely
  because they cannot be written earlier.
- Keep one hard, humbling practice alive in a spike — something with
  unfakeable feedback — so judgment stays calibrated and seniority doesn't
  curdle into pronouncement.

## Track B: Stewardship — compounding through others

- Shift teaching from individuals toward *institutions*: build or strengthen
  the course, curriculum, team, standard, open resource, or organization that
  teaches after you stop. One good institution outlearns any individual.
- Mentor successors deliberately — not just juniors, but the specific people
  who could carry your best work further than you will. Give the work away
  while you're still around to help.
- Fund, sponsor, or clear the path for young people the way (you now realize)
  someone once did for you.

## Track C: Reinvention — the deliberately new thing

Not everyone takes this track, but keep it honestly open:

- **A third spike, chosen freely.** For the first time, choose a field with
  *zero* career justification allowed — the criteria is pull. The learning
  habits are now three decades strong; watch how fast they carry you compared
  to year 1.
- Beginner-hood is the practice here, not the price. Being publicly bad at
  something new — with teachers far younger than you — is the single best defense
  against intellectual rigidity, which is this phase's real enemy.
- Some people's Track C becomes their most significant work. Careers
  regularly produce their best output after year 25; the agenda's job is to
  make sure yours has the option.

## Ongoing maintenance (all of Decade 3)

- **Habits, unchanged:** spaced repetition, weekly log, annual review. They
  are decades old now; they cost almost nothing and are load-bearing.
- **Update the map:** fields you mastered in Decade 1 have moved. Schedule
  periodic "re-foundations" — go back to school on your own field's last
  ten years, ideally taught by the generation that built them.
- **Cognitive health is curriculum:** exercise, sleep, social engagement, and
  sustained hard learning itself are the best-evidenced protectors of the
  instrument this whole agenda runs on. They stay on the schedule.

## Milestones (looser by design)

- [ ] One major synthesis work completed and shared.
- [ ] At least one institution/resource/successor carrying your work forward
      without your involvement.
- [ ] Something begun after year 21 at which you were a genuine beginner.
- [ ] All 44 ledger domains checked — the map covered once.
- [ ] The annual review of year 30 written, and Decade 4 drafted. Forty
      years left, and a completed map to work from. The agenda doesn't end
      here; it gets more interesting to write.

## Failure modes to watch for in Decade 3

- **Eminence trap** — spending the decade giving opinions about work instead
  of doing any. The humbling-practice rule in Track A is the antidote.
- **Vault-keeping** — deferring transfer ("I'll write it all down eventually")
  until the knowledge leaves with you. Stewardship is scheduled, not
  eventual.
- **Nostalgia curriculum** — teaching the field as it was in your prime.
  Reverse-mentoring and re-foundations keep the material current.
- **Coasting disguised as balance** — legitimate slowing down chosen
  consciously is fine; the failure mode is drift that was never chosen.
  The annual review is where the difference gets written down.
