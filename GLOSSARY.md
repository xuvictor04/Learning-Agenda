# Glossary — The System's Vocabulary

This repo invents some terms and repurposes others, and the words are not
decoration: each one marks a distinction the system actually runs on. A
literacy pass is not "reading about a field," a track is not a slot, and the
frontier slot is not the curiosity budget — confuse any of those pairs and
the machinery stops working in a specific, predictable way.

Entries are alphabetical, each with the file where the term is defined in
full. Entries beginning with *the* are filed under the noun.

---

### anti-goal
*`templates/annual-plan.md` ("Explicitly NOT this year");
`templates/annual-review.md`; `03-spine.md`, reordering rule 4*

Something you have decided, in writing, not to learn. Two kinds: the one-year
deferral, listed in January so it stops nagging, and the permanent exclusion
a domain earns after being deferred four times. Both live on paper; an
anti-goal you merely feel is not one.

**Why it matters:** an undeclared no keeps costing attention — it reappears
every January and competes silently with what you actually chose. Writing it
down converts an open loop into a closed decision, which is the only way
concurrency caps survive contact with an interesting book.

### artifact
*`01-principles.md`, principle 3; `resources/README.md`*

A finished thing produced by study: an essay, a proof, a program, a
performance, an object, a published explanation of one to three thousand
words. It is the unit that earns a checkmark anywhere in this system — no
artifact, no checkmark, regardless of hours spent.

**Why it matters:** it replaces an unanswerable question with an answerable
one. "Did I learn it?" has no honest answer and flatters everyone; "did I
finish it?" is unfakeable. Every other available measure — hours, pages,
courses, streaks — measures input, and input is what people already have too
much confidence in.

### bridge
*`resources/bridges.md`*

A pairing of two domains held in one head and pursued as a project. A bridge
qualifies only when you can state a specific question neither field alone can
answer — one a specialist in A would punt to B, and a specialist in B would
punt to A.

**Why it matters:** it separates a real intersection from interdisciplinary
enthusiasm. Themes, resonances, and "these both involve networks" are moods;
they produce conference panels and no results. The one-sentence question is
the test, and it also protects against the crank failure that literacy in two
fields makes very easy.

### canon
*`resources/README.md`; each cluster file's T3 entry*

Not great books in general, but a specific domain's load-bearing works — the
ones practitioners actually reference — with the parts worth reading named,
since most of them are not read cover to cover. Meeting the canon is a
required slot in every literacy pass, alongside a survey.

**Why it matters:** a survey tells you what a field concluded; the canon
shows you how it argues. Read only surveys and you end up holding a field's
results with no access to its mind, which is precisely the knowledge that
does not transfer to a new problem.

### concurrency cap
*`02-map.md`*

The per-moment limit on funded fields: at most one or two at T1, two or three
at T2, and roughly two new T3 literacies a year. The caps constrain
simultaneity, not lifetime totals — across seventy years they still permit
three or four masteries, a dozen or more working depths, and the full ledger
walked more than once.

**Why it matters:** the binding constraint is attention, not ambition, and
the two feel the same in January. Without a cap, breadth degrades into
surveying everything and mastering nothing — the exact failure the map is
built against.

### craft track
*`03-spine.md`; `resources/made-applied.md`*

The one ledger domain deliberately not given a year: a craft done with the
hands, started in year 3 and run continuously to about year 30, reaching
working-craftsperson level rather than literacy.

**Why it matters:** embodied skill cannot be brought to literacy in a hundred
hours and then left. It decays without contact, and it is the one entry on
the map that reading cannot fake — a description of how a plane cuts is not
adjacent to being able to use one. That is what makes it a track rather than
a slot.

### curiosity budget
*`02-map.md`; `templates/annual-plan.md`*

Ten to twenty percent of learning hours spent on whatever pulls this month:
off-ledger, no tier, no artifact, no accounting, no promotion path.

**Why it matters:** it's the only part of the system with no obligation
attached, and that is what keeps the rest from turning joy into bookkeeping.
It also works as a diagnostic in the other direction — if it ever hits zero,
the agenda is dying, and the repo treats that as the canary rather than as
evidence of focus.

### deliberate practice
*`01-principles.md`, principle 1; `resources/fundamentals.md`*

Practice at the edge of your ability, feedback-rich, and targeted at an
isolated weak sub-skill rather than at repeating the whole performance.
Distinct from exposure: reading and lectures are orientation, and the working
ratio is at least two hours of doing per hour of consuming.

**Why it matters:** time is not the input that skill responds to. Twenty
years of undeliberate practice produces twenty years of the same performance,
which is why experienced people plateau and why "I've been doing this for a
decade" predicts so little.

### frontier slot
*`04-frontier.md`*

A permanent rolling allocation of five to ten percent of learning hours —
roughly 25 to 50 a year, every year for seventy — reserved for things not on
the map. Contents change continuously; the slot never moves. It has a
mandatory output: an annual written list of what you looked at, what you
concluded, and what changed since last year.

**Why it matters:** it is not the curiosity budget. That one is for pull and
pleasure; this is deliberate reconnaissance against an asymmetric risk.
Missing a fad costs nothing; missing a genuine shift costs a decade.

### fundamentals
*`02-map.md`; `resources/fundamentals.md`*

The eight universal capacities the whole map runs on: writing clearly,
reading difficult material fast, math and statistics at a working level,
speaking, programming and data literacy, working with AI tools, memory
technique, and personal finance. Not domains — the instrument that the
domains get played on.

**Why it matters:** a weak fundamental taxes all 44 domains rather than one.
A slow reader pays that tax everywhere, forever. This is why years 1–2 are
disproportionately about the instrument, and why that front-loading has
repaid itself by roughly year 6.

### half-life (of knowledge)
*`01-principles.md`, principle 5*

How long a piece of knowledge stays useful. Three bands: decades and up
(mathematics, statistics, writing, physics, economics, the history of your
field), five to ten years (a field's core paradigms and major methods), one to
three years (tools, APIs, product versions, trends).

**Why it matters:** it converts "what should I learn?" into a scheduling
rule. Long half-life gets learned deeply and early from the best sources;
short half-life gets learned just-in-time inside a project and never appears
in a seventy-year plan. `04-frontier.md` exists because that rule has one
blind spot: paradigm shifts arrive disguised as tools.

### inspectional reading
*`resources/fundamentals.md`*

Adler's second level of reading: systematic skimming to establish what a book
is, what it claims, and whether it deserves close reading. The competence
target is inspecting a 400-page nonfiction book in an hour and stating its
thesis, structure, and main evidence.

**Why it matters:** selectivity, not speed, is what actually scales reading
throughput — most books deserve an hour rather than ten, and knowing which
is which within ten minutes of opening one is the skill. Treating every book
as an analytical read is the commonest reason an ambitious reading plan
stalls.

### kill criteria
*`templates/annual-plan.md`; checked in `templates/annual-review.md`*

Written in January for each of the year's big investments: the specific
result by June that would make you stop or change course.

**Why it matters:** the decision is cheap in advance and nearly impossible in
the moment, when sunk cost, identity, and whatever you told people are all
pulling one way. Deciding in January is admin; deciding in June is war. The
review then asks two questions — did they fire, and did you obey them —
because writing criteria you then ignore is worse than not writing them.

### the ledger
*`02-map.md`*

The 44-domain checklist of human knowledge, grouped into eight clusters plus
languages. Every unchecked box is a T3 target; a box gets checked when its
literacy pass is done. Open at the bottom — new domains can join it.

**Why it matters:** it makes "everything" finite and inspectable, and a list
whose end you can see behaves completely differently from an ambition. It
also carries the fact most lifetime-learning plans get wrong: it completes
around year 30, at fifty, with forty years still to run.

### literacy pass
*`02-map.md`; `resources/README.md`*

The standard T3 treatment of a domain: one survey work, one canonical work,
one published artifact, at roughly 100 to 300 hours. It is the unit of ledger
progress, and the same procedure runs a re-foundation and a frontier
candidate's trial.

**Why it matters:** it fixes the meaning of "I've studied that." Without a
defined pass, literacy drifts toward whatever quantity of reading made you
feel comfortable, usually a book and a half — and the ledger stops meaning
anything the moment its checkmarks stop meaning the same thing.

### the live layer
*`05-current.md`*

Current awareness: news, journals, data releases, and preprints, run at two
to four hours a week across a frequency ladder deliberately weighted toward
the annual tier.

**Why it matters:** everything else in the repo optimizes for durability, and
that bias has one hole — a map with no live layer becomes a museum, and you
end up knowing how the world works and not what it is doing. It is distinct
from the frontier slot: this one tracks events, that one tracks structural
change, and the frontier slot loses whenever the two are merged.

### the long shelf
*`resources/README.md`; the fourth block of every domain entry*

What a domain offers past T1: its subfields as separate entry points, the
further canon, a re-foundation watch, a lifetime practice, and rabbit holes.

**Why it matters:** the three tiers cover about ten years and this plan runs
seventy. A domain promoted in your fifties would otherwise run out of
material by your sixties. The long shelf is what makes forty more years
inside one field a real option rather than a re-read — "physics" is not one
territory but eight.

### maintenance
*`resources/languages.md`; `03-spine.md`; `phases/decade-4-integration.md`*

The recurring cost of holding something already acquired. For a language at
B2 or above: five to ten minutes of daily SRS on its deck plus an hour of
input a week. For the craft: continued contact. For the body in the later
decades: exercise, sleep, hearing and vision correction, moved from
background into scheduled curriculum.

**Why it matters:** acquisition gets planned and maintenance gets forgotten,
which is how people accumulate four languages they can read and none they can
speak. Everything acquired arrives with a bill; count it before adding the
next thing.

### the map
*`02-map.md`*

Both the file and the thing it describes: 44 domains in eight clusters, three
depth tiers, the fundamentals table, the concurrency caps, and the
guardrails. Deliberately general — it does not know or care what you already
know, and it doesn't shrink because you're ahead in places.

**Why it matters:** it separates territory from route. The map says what
exists; `03-spine.md` says what order you walk it in and why. Collapse the
two and every reordering starts to feel like a violation rather than the
ordinary choice it is.

### mode
*`resources/modes.md`*

A channel of learning: recorded courses, live classes, primary documents,
data, video, audio, museums and places, people, doing the thing,
conversation and teaching, reference works, AI, and fiction about a field.
The design rule is at least three modes per domain.

**Why it matters:** each mode fails differently. Books make you feel you
understand mechanisms you couldn't operate; video, results you couldn't
derive; doing gives skill without theory. Understanding forms where two modes
disagree or where one exposes what another skipped, which makes three modes
triangulation rather than variety.

### π-shape and T-shape
*`01-principles.md`, principle 4*

Shapes of a competence profile. A **T** is one deep spike standing on broad
fundamentals — the target for years 1–10. A **π** is two spikes, the second
grown in years 11–20 and chosen for combinatorial value with the first rather
than on interest alone.

**Why it matters:** the second stroke is where original work usually comes
from, because few people hold both fields at once. A T-shaped person competes
inside a crowded interior; a π-shaped one owns an intersection almost nobody
else can occupy. Choosing the second spike for combination rather than
enthusiasm is the entire trick.

### placement
*`05-current.md`, section 7*

The test that a literacy has held: when a story touches a domain you took to
T3, you can name the sub-field, the standing questions, and why this is or
isn't surprising.

**Why it matters:** it turns news from consumption into a free, continuous
audit of the map. Failing to place a story is not ignorance about the
story — it is the signal that a domain has decayed into a memory of having
studied it, which makes placement the cheapest re-foundation trigger the
system has.

### promotion
*`02-map.md`; `04-frontier.md`, section 4*

Moving a field to a deeper tier (T3 to T2, T2 to T1), or moving a frontier
candidate onto the map as a real domain. It happens only at an annual review,
only after the literacy artifact exists, and never on enthusiasm.

**Why it matters:** it puts a deliberate gate between excitement and
commitment, and a tier is a multi-year budget rather than a mood. Excitement
is also the precise emotion a hype cycle is engineered to produce, so the
decision belongs to the version of you holding a calendar, not the one
holding a new book.

### rabbit hole
*`resources/README.md`; each domain's long shelf*

A narrow topic inside a domain worth about a month, listed as material for
the curiosity budget rather than the ledger. No tier, no artifact, no
promotion path.

**Why it matters:** it gives depth-hunger a legitimate destination that isn't
a tier promotion. Wanting to spend four weeks on one strange corner of a
field is a healthy impulse and a terrible reason to commit three years, and
the system needs somewhere to put the difference.

### re-foundation
*`04-frontier.md`, section 6; `phases/decade-4-integration.md`*

Redoing a domain's literacy pass on current material — a survey published in
the last three years, a current primary source, a new artifact — at perhaps
half the original hours, because the scaffolding is still there. Run roughly
every 15 to 20 years per domain, or sooner on trigger.

**Why it matters:** it distinguishes forgetting from the field having moved.
You didn't lose the geology you learned in 1955; plate tectonics arrived. The
triggers are behavioral, not calendrical: you can no longer follow a current
talk, or the methods sections read like a foreign language.

### reverse-mentoring
*`04-frontier.md`, section 8; `phases/decade-5-reinvention.md`*

Arranging to be taught, as a student rather than as a senior person listening
graciously, by someone twenty or thirty years younger in a domain where they
are ahead of you. One a year from your forties on, on the calendar.

**Why it matters:** the seniority trap is structural, not attitudinal. You
get consulted about what you already know, rewarded for the depth you have,
and gradually stop being asked questions you can't answer. Intending to stay
open does not fix an incentive; a scheduled appointment does.

### spaced repetition (SRS)
*`01-principles.md`, principle 2; `resources/fundamentals.md`*

A review schedule timed just before you would forget, run daily at fifteen to
twenty minutes on cards you wrote from your own study in your own words.
Lifelong infrastructure rather than an exam technique — downloaded decks
teach recognition, not understanding.

**Why it matters:** breadth is unretainable without it. Forty-four domains at
literacy depth is a large factual surface, and the forgetting curve quietly
reclaims nearly all of it, leaving you having read everything and knowing
nothing. It is also the cheapest habit here and the one whose payoff is
furthest away, which is why it dies first.

### spike
*`01-principles.md`, principle 4; `03-spine.md`*

A field taken to T1 and carrying your professional weight. The first is built
in decade 1, the second in decade 2, and an optional third in decade 5 chosen
on pull alone with zero career justification permitted. In any conflict
between the spike and the ledger, the ledger yields.

**Why it matters:** it's what keeps breadth from being dilettantism. Depth in
one field is what makes the other forty-three literacies usable — you know
what real depth feels like from inside, so you can tell when you don't have
it. A polymath with no spike is an audience member.

### the spine
*`03-spine.md`*

The linear sequence: which domains in which year, with the prerequisite
argument attached to each, from year 1 to year 70. A default sequence, not a
contract — reorderable at an annual review under five stated rules.

**Why it matters:** the map answers "what is there?" and `resources/` answers
"with what?", but only the spine answers "what do I do this year, in what
order, and what has to come first so the thing after it isn't wasted?" That
last question is the one that actually stops people, and it's why the file
exists separately from the map.

### syntopical reading
*`resources/fundamentals.md`*

Adler's fourth and highest level: reading many books on one question and
constructing your own framework — including a statement of where the authors
actually disagree, in terms none of them used.

**Why it matters:** it is the skill this entire repo depends on. Literacy
passes, artifacts, bridges, and the later synthesis work are all syntopical
operations, and the capacity is what turns a completed ledger from a
collection of book summaries into a point of view. It is also the thing a
summary of a book cannot give you.

### T1 / T2 / T3
*`02-map.md`*

The three depth tiers, defined as time budgets rather than rankings. **T1**,
mastery: contribute original work and hold your own with the field's best,
five to ten years. **T2**, working depth: do real work, read the primary
literature, teach the fundamentals, one to three years. **T3**, literacy:
state the field's core questions and big ideas and follow an expert, 100 to
300 hours.

**Why it matters:** no field is inherently T1 or T3, and any field can be
aimed at any tier or re-aimed later. The tier is a decision about your
budget, which is what makes "everything" arithmetic instead of aspiration.

### track versus slot
*`03-spine.md`*

A **slot** is a domain that occupies a year and is then checked off. A
**track** runs continuously and never ends: spaced repetition, the weekly
log, the language, the craft, the spike, current awareness, the curiosity
budget, and — despite its name — the frontier slot, which is a permanent
allocation rather than a year.

**Why it matters:** some things cannot be finished, and scheduling them as if
they could is exactly how they die. You do not complete a craft in year 3 and
move on; you keep touching it or you lose it. Deciding which of the two a
commitment is, before you start it, prevents most quiet abandonments.

### transmission
*`03-spine.md`, the second half; `phases/decade-5-reinvention.md`*

The fourth kind of work in the plan's second half: teaching, synthesis,
institutions, named successors, the book-length account, and an archive made
legible to a stranger.

**Why it matters:** unshared mastery doesn't compound; it retires. And it is
continuous with learning rather than a valedictory phase tacked on at the
end, which is why teaching starts in year 3 and not year 51: principle 6
treats explaining something to someone a level below you as the final exam,
not as a detour from studying.

### weekly log
*`templates/weekly-log.md`*

Five minutes, the same time every week, one row recording what you practiced
deliberately, what you built or wrote, what you read, and whether the
repetition streak held. An empty week logged as empty is a valid and useful
entry.

**Why it matters:** its job is latency, not accountability — making drift
visible in weeks rather than in years, which is the difference between a
correction and a post-mortem. It is also the restart mechanism: any lapsed
track resumes at the next weekly log, at half size if necessary, which keeps
a broken streak from becoming an abandoned agenda.

### working depth
*`02-map.md`; `resources/README.md`*

T2. You can do real work in the field, read its primary literature without an
intermediary, and teach its fundamentals — one to three years of investment,
held by two or three fields at a time.

**Why it matters:** this is the tier most people mean when they say "I know
that field," and the one most often claimed on the strength of a literacy
pass. The distinction is concrete rather than a matter of degree: literacy
lets you follow an expert, working depth lets you be useful to one, and only
the second survives being asked to do the work.
