# Decade 2 (Years 11–20): Completion and First Depth

**Mission:** finish the map, then start converting it. The 44-domain ledger
completes at **year 15**; the five years after it are the first time in the
plan that depth, not coverage, is the whole job.

## The event this decade is built around

**The ledger completes at year 15, with fifty-five years still to run.**

That is the single most important fact about this plan, and the one most
people get wrong when they imagine a lifetime of learning. Covering the map is
not the plan — it is the first fifth of it. At three domains a year the
whole territory of `02-map.md` is walked before the second spike is even
funded, which means the remaining fifty-five years are not a long tail of
mopping up. They are the actual work, and this decade is where the shift from
one to the other happens.

Be precise about what year 15 buys you: not expertise in forty-four fields,
but the ability to read into anything, to see where two fields touch, and to
know which stranger to call. That is a starting position, not a trophy.

## Years 11–15: The last fourteen domains

**Themes**

- Fourteen domains remain. Three a year through year 14, the last two in
  year 15, and the map is covered. Hold the pace — the temptation in these
  years is to slow down because the end is visible and the spike is getting
  interesting. Finish the ledger cleanly; a map with six holes in it is not
  a map you can synthesize from.
- The domains left at this point are mostly the applied and the human ones,
  and they read differently now. You are bringing a decade of professional
  judgment to them rather than a student's attention, and the artifacts
  should show it.
- Keep the first spike compounding through all of it: real stakes, real
  post-mortems, and the move from *solving problems* to *choosing problems*.
  Taste in what's worth working on is the master-level skill, and it's built
  by post-mortems, studying your field's history of bets, and proximity to
  people with proven taste.
- **Run the completion review at year 15 properly** — see
  `templates/annual-review.md`. Go row by row through the ledger and mark,
  honestly, which passes were real and which were tourism. A domain you
  cannot now state the core questions of is unchecked, whatever the box says.
  Redo it in year 16.

**Milestones by year 15**

- [ ] **All 44 ledger domains checked**, each with an artifact you published.
- [ ] The completion review written, with every weak pass named and
      rescheduled rather than quietly kept.
- [ ] The craft and the language tracks still running — they were never
      ledger rows, and they don't stop when the rows do.
- [ ] Written answer to the question the completed map now poses: *which
      three or four of these would I still be reading in twenty years?*

## Years 16–20: The first real depth

**Themes**

- **The ledger's hours are now free — roughly 450 a year.** They do not stay
  free by default; unallocated hours evaporate into other people's schedules
  inside six months. Allocate them at the year-16 review: most to depth, a
  share to the frontier slot (`04-frontier.md`), the curiosity budget
  untouched.
- **Promote three or four domains to working depth.** You hold fifteen years
  of evidence about which ones actually pulled — the ones you kept reading
  after the artifact was done, the ones that keep turning up in your spike's
  problems. One at a time, at annual reviews, under the tier rules in
  `02-map.md`. Promote for combination as much as affection: a domain that
  meets your spike at an angle beats two that sit off on their own.
- **The second spike moves from side allocation to funded commitment at
  year 16.** Chosen at the end of Decade 1, it has had foundations running
  quietly ever since; now it gets real hours and real projects — ideally ones
  that already use your first spike. This is the π advantage, and it arrives
  a full decade earlier than a four-hour week could ever have delivered it.
- Start saying no. Depth requires longer uninterrupted arcs than literacy
  ever did; protect 1–2 deep commitments per year over 6 shallow ones. The
  habit that got you through thirty literacy passes — many things, short
  arcs — is now the wrong habit, and it will not notice that it has become
  wrong.
- Run the **20-year audit** against `02-map.md`: which bets paid off, what
  you'd tell your year-1 self, what the next decade is about. Decade 3
  planning starts here, and it may legitimately point somewhere new.
- Begin deliberately learning from people two levels down: fields move, and
  the newest arrivals see the new thing first. Reverse-mentoring keeps your
  model of the field current when seniority starts insulating you from it.

**Milestones by end of the decade**

- [ ] 5–7 domains at working depth in total, counting Decade 1's.
- [ ] First spike at mastery — original work in it, not just competent work.
- [ ] Second spike at "trusted professional" level — you've shipped real work
      in it.
- [ ] First deliberately *hybrid* project completed at the intersection of
      the two spikes.
- [ ] Explicit personal answer to "what problems are worth my next five
      years?" — written down, revisited annually.
- [ ] 20-year audit written; Decade 3 direction drafted.
- [ ] Learning block still on the calendar, still defended, still used.

## Failure modes to watch for in Decade 2

- **Coasting on the completed map** — treating year 15 as an arrival. Forty-
  four literacy passes is a license to read, not a body of work. The five
  years after completion are where it either becomes something or doesn't.
- **Ledger withdrawal** — the three-a-year rhythm was a structure as much as
  a curriculum, and when it stops, the hours it occupied can vanish without
  anyone noticing. Allocate them on paper at year 16 or lose them.
- **Promotion sprawl** — the freed hours make it tempting to promote eight
  domains at once and reach depth in none. The concurrency caps exist for
  exactly this moment; hold them even though you can now afford to break them.
- **The comfortable plateau** — repeating year 12 eight times and calling it
  eight years of experience. Competence is comfortable, and by now you're paid
  to do what you're already good at.
- **Management amnesia** — moving into leadership and letting the craft
  atrophy completely. Keep ~20% hands-on practice in at least one spike;
  judgment decays without contact with the material.
- **Hoarding** — accumulating expertise without teaching or publishing it.
  Unshared mastery doesn't compound; it just retires with you.
- **Peak-earning capture** — every hour monetized, zero invested. The
  curiosity budget (see `02-map.md`) is the canary: when it hits zero, the
  agenda is dying.
