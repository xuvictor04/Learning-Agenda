# Decade 1 (Years 1–10): Foundations

**Mission:** leave this decade with (a) broad, durable fundamentals, (b) thirty
of the ledger's forty-four domains at literacy, (c) genuine professional depth
in your primary field, and (d) the lifelong habits that make every decade
after this one possible.

You are likely a student for the first part of this decade. That is an unfair
advantage: structured time, expert access, and cheap failure. Spend it on the
hardest, slowest-to-learn material — you will never again have conditions this
good for learning real analysis, organic chemistry, or classical rhetoric.

**Say the load out loud, because it is heavier than any earlier draft of this
plan assumed.** The budget is twenty-five hours a week, roughly 1,300 hours a
year, and it buys **three literacy domains a year** alongside the fundamentals
and a spike you are pushing to professional strength at the same time. Thirty
domains land in this decade — more than two-thirds of the ledger in the first
seventh of the plan. The trunk years run *above* the line rather than under
it, because they carry the fundamentals as well as the domains. When a year
cannot hold all of it, the ledger yields and the spike survives. That priority
never changes, in any decade.

## Years 1–4: Breadth + fundamentals (typically overlapping formal study)

**Themes**

- Three ledger domains a year from year 1, in the order `03-spine.md` gives
  or a deliberate reordering of it. Twelve domains checked by the end of
  year 4 — the load-bearing ones first, because everything downstream gets
  cheaper after them.
- Take the *hard, fundamental* version of every subject when there's a choice.
  Fundamentals compound for seventy years; easy electives compound for none.
  At this pace the fundamentals are not a side quest — a slow reader pays that
  tax three times a year now instead of twice.
- Build the three permanent habits now, while life is still simple:
  1. Daily spaced repetition (20–30 min — the vault is filling three times
     faster than the old pace assumed).
  2. Weekly log (5 min — see `templates/weekly-log.md`).
  3. One finished artifact per domain, plus one per term in your field.
- Write constantly. Start a public or semi-public set of notes/blog in year 1
  or 2. Quality is irrelevant at first; the habit and the archive are the point.
- Sample widely on purpose: take courses, jobs, or projects in 3–4 subfields
  of your primary field before committing to one.

**Milestones by end of year 4**

- [ ] 12 ledger domains checked, each with a published artifact.
- [ ] Math/stats foundation appropriate to your field completed (not just
      passed — usable).
- [ ] Can write a clear 2,000-word explanation of a technical topic.
- [ ] Spaced-repetition habit unbroken for 2+ years.
- [ ] 15+ finished artifacts, at least one you'd show a stranger.
- [ ] Shortlist of 2–3 subfields you'd consider going deep in.

## Years 5–7: Commit, go deep, and make the first promotions

**Themes**

- Choose your subfield/specialization. The choice matters less than the
  committing: depth in a good-enough subfield beats shallow sampling of the
  perfect one. Choose using evidence from years 1–4, then stop re-deciding.
- Shift ratio hard toward practice: real projects, research, internships,
  apprenticeship under people better than you. Seek the best available
  masters — the quality of who you learn *from* is now the biggest variable.
- Learn the *history and canon* of your field: the classic papers/works,
  the famous failures, how the field got here. This is what separates
  practitioners from people who merely took the courses.
- Begin teaching: tutor, TA, publish explanations of what you're learning.
- **The first promotions happen here, around year 6.** Fifteen literacy passes
  is enough evidence to see which domains you kept reading after the artifact
  was finished. Promote one to working depth at an annual review — inside the
  tier rules in `02-map.md`, never on enthusiasm — and hold the ledger at
  three a year while you do it.

**Milestones by end of year 7**

- [ ] 21 ledger domains checked.
- [ ] Recognized specialization: people around you would name you for it.
- [ ] One substantial project/thesis/publication/product you led.
- [ ] Read (not skimmed) 10+ canonical works of your field.
- [ ] Taught the equivalent of a semester of material to someone.
- [ ] At least one domain promoted to working depth at a review, with the
      reason written down.

## Years 8–10: Professional depth + choose the second spike

**Themes**

- Operate at professional level: real stakes, real users/patients/clients,
  real consequences. Learning now comes primarily from doing hard work and
  doing post-mortems on it.
- Close the fundamentals gaps that survived formal education — check the
  table in `02-map.md` and schedule the remaining weak rows. Do it now: every
  remaining domain is read through them.
- Choose the **second spike** (see `02-map.md` criteria) and begin its
  foundations at ~20% of spike time. Its real funding arrives at year 16,
  when the ledger closes and its hours come free; this is the planting, not
  the harvest.
- Build your first mentoring relationships in both directions: a mentor above
  you, someone junior below you.

**Milestones by end of the decade**

- [ ] 30 ledger domains checked — two-thirds of the map, in one decade.
- [ ] Two or three domains at working depth, each with work a specialist
      would recognize as real.
- [ ] Trusted with hard problems in your specialization without supervision.
- [ ] Second spike chosen, with its fundamental coursework/reading underway.
- [ ] Public body of work exists: portfolio, publications, talks, or a
      well-kept public notebook — something a stranger can evaluate.
- [ ] All three permanent habits (repetition, logging, annual review) intact
      for 5+ years.

## Failure modes to watch for in Decade 1

- **Credential tunnel vision** — optimizing grades/certificates over
  capability. The transcript stops mattering ~3 years out; the skills never do.
- **Tool chasing** — filling years with short-half-life tools (see
  principle 5) because they feel productive and are easy.
- **Serial sampling** — restarting in a new subfield every 18 months and
  reaching depth in none. The years 5–7 commitment exists to prevent this.
- **Pace over artifacts** — the specific failure of a three-domain year.
  Three surveys read and nothing written is zero domains, not three. No
  artifact, no checkmark, however much you read.
- **Spending the spike's hours on the ledger** — thirty domains is seductive
  because progress is visible and checkable. A completed map with no
  professional depth under it makes you an audience member. When the two
  conflict, the ledger yields.
- **Silent habit death** — the streak breaks in a hard semester and never
  restarts. Broken streaks are fine; unrestarted ones are how agendas die.
  The rule: any lapsed habit gets restarted at the next weekly log, at
  half size if necessary.
