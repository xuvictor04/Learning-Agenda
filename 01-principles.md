# Principles: How Learning Compounds Over Decades

These are the mechanics the whole agenda relies on. Everything in `phases/` and
`templates/` assumes you're applying them.

## 1. Deliberate practice, not exposure

Time spent ≠ skill gained. Skill grows from practice that is:

- **At the edge of your ability** — hard enough that you fail sometimes.
- **Feedback-rich** — you find out quickly whether you got it right
  (tests, code that runs, a teacher's critique, a market's response).
- **Targeted** — you isolate the weak sub-skill instead of repeating the
  whole performance.

Reading and watching lectures are *orientation*, not practice. Budget them
accordingly: for most skills, aim for at least 2 hours of doing per hour of
consuming.

## 2. Spaced repetition for anything worth remembering

Memory decays on a predictable curve, and reviewing just before you forget
flattens it. For factual knowledge (vocabulary, formulas, definitions,
anatomy, syntax, case law — whatever your field's atoms are), use a spaced
repetition system (Anki or similar) as lifelong infrastructure:

- Add cards *from your own study*, in your own words. Downloaded decks teach
  recognition, not understanding.
- 15–20 minutes a day sustains a vault of tens of thousands of facts
  indefinitely. This is the single highest-leverage habit in this agenda.

## 3. Projects are the unit of deep learning

Courses give you a map; projects force you to walk the territory. Every year
of this agenda is anchored on **artifacts** — things you build, write, prove,
perform, or publish — because:

- A project surfaces the gaps a course lets you skip.
- Finished artifacts compound into a portfolio, reputation, and evidence.
- "Did I finish it?" is unfakeable, unlike "did I learn it?"

Rule of thumb: never study a subject for more than a month without a concrete
artifact that the study is *for*.

## 4. The T-shape, then the π-shape

- **Years 1–10:** build one deep spike (your primary field) atop broad
  fundamentals — a **T**.
- **Years 11–20:** grow a second spike in an adjacent or complementary field —
  a **π**. Most original work happens where two competencies intersect,
  because few people hold both.
- **Years 21+:** synthesis across spikes is your comparative advantage.

Pick the second spike for *combinatorial value* with the first, not just
interest: e.g. biology + machine learning, law + software, design +
manufacturing, economics + energy systems.

## 5. Fundamentals age slowly; tools age fast

Rank learning investments by half-life:

| Half-life | Examples | Investment style |
|-----------|----------|------------------|
| Decades+ | Math, statistics, writing, physics, economics, psychology, rhetoric, history of your field | Learn deeply, from the best sources, early |
| ~5–10 yrs | Core paradigms of your field, major languages/frameworks/methods | Learn thoroughly when needed |
| ~1–3 yrs | Specific tools, APIs, product versions, trends | Learn just-in-time, shallowly |

A decade-scale agenda front-loads the top row. Tools are learned inside
projects, on demand, and never scheduled years in advance.

## 6. Teaching is the final exam

You don't fully understand something until you can explain it to someone a
level below you. From year 3 onward this agenda schedules regular teaching:
notes published online, tutoring, talks, mentoring, workshops. Teaching is
not a detour from learning — it's the highest rung of it, and it compounds
your network and reputation as a side effect.

## 7. Meta-learning: review the system itself

Twice a year, ask:

- What did I learn fastest this period, and what conditions made that true?
- What did I abandon, and was abandoning it right?
- Is my current field allocation (see `02-map.md`) still what I'd choose today?

Quitting a line of study deliberately, in an annual review, is discipline.
Quitting by silently drifting away is the failure mode this repo exists to
prevent.

## 8. Protect the physical substrate

Sleep, exercise, and mental health are learning infrastructure, not
competition for study time. Sleep is when memory consolidates; chronic
under-sleeping to study more is borrowing from the exact resource you're
trying to build. A sustainable 10 focused hours/week beats an unsustainable
30 every time — this agenda is thirty years long, and burnout is its most
common cause of death.
